Data Annotation: The Hidden Work Behind Every AI Model

By Tabish Syed • 2 mins read
Scroll

AI models don’t learn from raw data alone. Most of them learn from data that a person has labeled first. That labeling work is called data annotation, and it decides how good the final model can be.

What annotation looks like

For an image model, annotators draw boxes around objects or mark which pixels belong to a road, a person or a leaf. For text, they tag names, topics or the feeling behind a sentence. For video, they describe what happens and when it happens.

My own experience

I work as a data annotator and have annotated over 5,000 minutes of robotics video on the Encord platform. My job was to write captions that describe the actions a robot performs, both for a whole task and for each short segment of it. The captions are used to train robot learning and computer vision models.

What I learned

Consistency matters more than speed. If two annotators describe the same action in two different ways, the model sees a contradiction. That is why every project has written guidelines, and why annotators follow the same style even when another phrasing would also be correct.

Edge cases take the most thought. A robot hand that starts a movement and stops halfway, or a scene where two actions overlap, has no obvious label. Reviewing these cases carefully keeps the dataset reliable.

Why it matters

A model can only learn what its labels teach it. Wrong or inconsistent labels become wrong or inconsistent predictions. Careful annotation is slow, quiet work, but every accurate AI system rests on it.
